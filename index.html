How the scoring works

Each finished session counts as one of these:

  • Good day: a green day where every exercise was fine (pain 3/10 or less).
  • Held steady: an amber or red day, or one exercise was sore, above 3/10. Counts neither way.
  • Flare-up: any exercise was much worse (sharp pain). You lose one good day, and the most you can do tomorrow is amber.

Collect 6 good days to go to the next stage. There are 5 stages, from calming the tendon to full activity. Held-steady days are fine. You add load only when your elbow handles the current load well.

That's at least 30 sessions. Tendons adapt slowly. Some people recover in a few months. For many people, recovery takes more than a year, and the pain often returns. Keep doing strength work after you finish. The weekly grip check shows progress before the pain stops fully.

Protecting the wrists day to day
  • Your ligaments are lax, so the muscles must hold the joint. Don't stretch the wrists to the end of their range.
  • Do push-ups and planks on your fists or on handles, with the wrist straight.
  • On slopers, pinches, side pulls and underclings, lock the wrist straight before you pull. Don't let it move under load.
  • A soft click with no pain is common in lax joints. If a click causes pain, use a lighter load or keep the wrist straighter.
  • Wrist tape or a wrist band can feel supportive during hard sessions. No study shows that it prevents injury, so it does not replace the training.
  • Continue your elbow strength work 2–3 times a week. Switch plans with the buttons at the top.
Protecting the elbow day to day
  • Lift with the palm up, or with both hands, instead of palm down at arm's length.
  • Use thicker handles and a looser grip on tools, rackets, mice and bags.
  • Make short pauses during long gripping or typing.
  • A forearm strap worn during the activities that hurt can ease pain at the time.
  • Keep moving: complete rest weakens the tendon. Reduce what hurts, don't stop everything.
  • When you return to your sport, first practice its movements at low effort (for example, easy climbs). A coach can help you correct the technique that strains your elbow.

        Not medical advice. This is an educational self-management tool. It does not replace a doctor or physiotherapist. See a clinician if a click comes with sharp pain, swelling or a weak grip, or if the wrist suddenly fails under load. Also see a clinician if wrist pain lasts more than 3 weeks, if it starts after a fall onto the hand, if you feel a painful snap when you turn the palm up, or if you have numbness or tingling. Small ligament tears and some fractures hurt only a little at first.See a clinician if the pain is sharp, comes at night or gets worse over weeks. Also see a clinician if you have numbness, tingling or neck pain. If you see no progress after some months, ask a clinician about other treatments, such as shockwave therapy. Cortisone shots do not help long term and can weaken the tendon. Surgery is rarely necessary. Clinicians consider it only after 6–12 months of other treatment with no improvement.
